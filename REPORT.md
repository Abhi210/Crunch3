# Method Description

<!-- Describe the algorithm used to generate the predicted scores. (5-10 sentences) -->
We predict the twelve thermogenic signature z-scores for each candidate gene pair as a fixed, rank-normalized blend of two components, each computed per single gene and then composed across the pair. The first component estimates a gene's effect as the mean expression shift (delta) it produces relative to control cells, averaged over the top-5,000 most variable genes in the single-gene perturbation screen and then projected onto each of the twelve thermogenic signature gene sets; for the 122 genes that were actually perturbed this delta is the true observed average, and for the remaining test genes it is a cosine-similarity-weighted average of the five most similar perturbed genes' observed deltas, with similarity measured in a pretrained single-cell transformer's gene-embedding space. The second component is an ensemble of eight small symmetric residual networks that map a pair of gene embeddings to twelve z-scores; it is trained to reproduce the experimentally measured single- and double-gene scores, and any test pair that reduces to one of the 133 experimentally measured single genes has its score overridden with that gene's exact measured z-scores rather than a network prediction. Double-gene composition differs by component: the mean-delta component is purely additive in delta-expression space with no synergy term, while the network ensemble adds a learned residual on top of the additive sum of its own single-gene predictions, so any pairwise synergy in the blend comes only from that residual. Each component's twelve per-signature scores are independently rank-normalized to [0,1] across all 4,474,413 candidate pairs and combined as a fixed weighted sum (weight 0.54 on the mean-delta component, 0.46 on the network ensemble), with the weights chosen to maximize held-out double-gene Spearman correlation. FinalAggScore is the mean of the three largest of the twelve blended z-scores per pair, and the output ranks all pairs by this value in descending order. The output is the required 15-column table (GenePairID, the twelve signature scores, FinalAggScore, Rank) over all 4,474,413 candidate pairs.

# Design rationale

<!-- Explain the rationale behind the proposed gene panel design. (5-10 sentences) -->
The panel is scored by the Spearman correlation of FinalAggScore over millions of candidate pairs, the large majority of which involve a gene that was never directly perturbed, so the central design problem is estimating a single gene's effect well enough to generalize and then composing pairs from those estimates. We use a direct, parameter-free mean-delta estimator for the generalization component rather than a learned generative model of perturbation responses: a compositional latent-variable autoencoder for this purpose was the original design intent, but is no longer available in the current single-cell modeling toolchain we use, and a direct average of the observed knockout-versus-control expression difference reconstructs the same training-gene signal with no additional fitted parameters and therefore no extra overfitting risk on only 122 labeled genes. For genes that were never perturbed, this delta is proxied by a similarity-weighted average of the five most similar perturbed genes in a pretrained single-cell transformer's embedding space, on the assumption that genes with similar global expression context produce similar perturbation responses. We also evaluated a third candidate signal - the embedding shift produced by in-silico removing a gene's token from a different pretrained single-cell transformer and re-encoding the cell - but rejected it after validation: across twenty probe genes spanning a range of token-survival rates, only one showed a silencing effect distinguishable from ordinary cell-to-cell noise, and a paired bootstrap confidence interval on the resulting change in held-out Spearman included zero, so it was dropped rather than shipped at a small weight, consistent with a rule that every candidate component must show a measurable, validated benefit before inclusion. The blend weight between the two retained components was tuned directly against held-out double-gene Spearman correlation, and scores are rank-normalized per component before blending because the two components have different raw scales and a rank-based combination keeps a simple fixed-weight linear blend well-behaved across both. One limitation we want to state plainly: the only labeled double-gene pairs available for this held-out check come from a small 15-gene combinatorial screen, and the network-ensemble component's residual was itself fit using those same pairs, so the reported double-gene Spearman is optimistic for the truly novel gene pairs that dominate the test set; we report this caveat directly rather than presenting the held-out number as an unqualified generalization estimate.

# Data and Resources

<!-- Describe the datasets, prior knowledge, and any additional resources used. (5-10 sentences) -->
Training labels are the perturbation-level mean thermogenic z-scores from two provided tables: a single-gene transcription-factor knockout screen (TF150_ThermoScores_perturbation.csv, 122 usable single-gene rows after deduplication) and a 15-gene combinatorial screen (TF15_MOI2_ThermoScores_perturbation.csv) containing single and double knockouts, with the combinatorial screen taking precedence on overlapping genes. The mean-delta component is computed directly from TF150.h5ad: control and knockout cells are identified from the screen's perturbation labels, expression is restricted to the top-5,000 highly variable genes, and each gene's delta is the mean expression of its knockout cells minus the mean expression of control cells, projected onto the twelve signature gene sets defined in thermogenic_signatures.csv. Gene-similarity weighting for novel genes, and the gene embeddings used by the network-ensemble component, both come from the pretrained scGPT whole-human single-cell foundation model's encoder embedding matrix, L2-normalized, with out-of-vocabulary genes assigned the mean embedding; this is a third-party pretrained checkpoint, not an additional labeled dataset. The candidate set of 4,474,413 gene pairs to score is read from predict_perturbations_3.parquet exactly as provided. All artifacts the submitted code loads at inference time - the precomputed per-gene signature deltas and their normalization statistics, the row-order index mapping genes to those deltas, and the network-ensemble checkpoint (which itself bundles its own copy of the scGPT embedding matrix and the experimentally measured single-gene scores used for the override) - are included in the self-contained resources directory. No external single-cell datasets, regulatory or protein-interaction networks, or literature-derived gene sets were used beyond the competition-provided files and the one pretrained foundation-model checkpoint. The pipeline is implemented in NumPy, pandas, and PyTorch.
